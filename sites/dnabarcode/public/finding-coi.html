Finding the COI region

The barcode used here is the "Folmer region": roughly the first 650 bases of the mitochondrial COI gene, the stretch amplified by the widely used primers LCO1490 and HCO2198.

If you sequenced a specimen

If your lab used standard COI barcoding primers, your sequence is already the barcode. Trim low-quality ends, join the forward and reverse reads into one sequence if you have both, and paste the result.

If you have a longer sequence

A whole mitochondrial genome or a long contig is fine: paste all of it (up to 20,000 bases). The service slides along the sequence, finds the stretch that looks most like the COI barcode, and tells you where it was found.

If you have a GenBank record

In the record's features, look for a gene or CDS named COX1, COI or CO1. Copy that stretch as DNA (not the protein translation), or simply paste the whole mitochondrial genome.

Checking a sequence first

If in doubt, NCBI BLAST or the BOLD Systems identification engine will show whether a sequence is COI. dnabarcode.org also checks: if it doesn't find COI, it says so instead of guessing.
